# Literature Matrix v0.1

## Scope

This matrix identifies the minimum literature required to position V-COF reinstantiation without overstating novelty. It distinguishes established work, recent preprints and the specific gap addressed by the proposed article.

## Memory architectures and benchmarks

| Work | Main contribution | What it evaluates | Relevance to V-COF | Remaining gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Park et al. (2023), *Generative Agents* | Memory stream, reflection and planning for believable agents | Behavioral believability and component ablations | Establishes memory/reflection as agent architecture | Does not govern cross-instance authority, canonical precedence or false identity claims |
| Packer et al. (2023), *MemGPT* | Hierarchical virtual-context management | Long-document analysis and multi-session chat | Demonstrates explicit memory-tier management | Focuses on context capacity rather than legitimate role resumption |
| Wu et al. (2024/2025), *LongMemEval* | Five long-term memory abilities | Extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, updates and abstention | Strong baseline for persistent-memory evaluation | Does not directly test jurisdiction, provenance, authority or reinstantiation legitimacy |
| Samuel et al. (2024), *PersonaGym* | Dynamic persona evaluation and PersonaScore | Persona adherence across many personas and questions | Supports separate measurement of persona performance | Persona is not primarily modeled as bounded operational jurisdiction |
| Hu et al. (2025), *MemoryAgentBench* | Incremental multi-turn memory benchmark | Retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding and forgetting | Useful for memory-component diagnostics | Does not test continuity claims or human ratification |
| Shen et al. (2026), *EvolMem* | Cognitive multi-session memory benchmark | Declarative and non-declarative memory dimensions | Broadens memory taxonomy | Memory ability remains distinct from governed continuation |
| Xu et al. (2026), *MemGym* | Long-horizon agentic memory environment | Dialogue, research, coding and computer-use regimes | Brings memory evaluation into real agent execution | V-COF adds authority, evidence and continuity-specific gates |
| Dabas et al. (2026), *MEMDRIFT* | Memory-induced tool-call drift | Bias leakage from stored memories into tool parameters | Directly motivates relevance and jurisdiction gates | Does not provide a complete reinstantiation protocol |

## Philosophy of continuity

| Work | Relevant concept | V-COF use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoemaker (1970), *Persons and Their Pasts* | Quasi-memory and causal access to past experience | Basis for provenance-bearing inherited operational records | Used analogically; no claim that machine records are human episodic memories |
| Parfit (1971; 1984) | Psychological connectedness and continuity; identity may not be what matters | Motivates functional continuity without numerical identity | V-COF makes an engineering and governance claim, not a metaphysical proof |

## Defensible novelty claim

The article must not claim to introduce agent memory, persona evaluation, multi-session dialogue or abstention.

Its proposed contribution is the integrated treatment of:

1. reinstantiation as a governed state transition;
2. explicit denial of numerical identity across instances;
3. operational quasi-memory with provenance and non-autobiographical language;
4. persona as jurisdiction, authority and obligation rather than style alone;
5. canonical precedence among contradictory inherited records;
6. Propose & Ratify as an authority model;
7. fail-closed behavior when ratification, evidence or canonical state is missing;
8. operational trust as warranted, domain-specific reliance;
9. evaluation of false continuity claims and unauthorized continuation.
